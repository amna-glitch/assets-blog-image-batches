What creates variety at scale

Mode by post type

Different kinds of posts get different kinds of covers: a statistic for data posts, a product shot for launches, a person for stories.

Stripe · Rippling · Calendly · Ramp

Palette by category

Each category owns a colour from the same family, so the grid alternates without breaking the brand.

Mercury · Stripe · Zocdoc

Real, varied photography

Documentary-style photos where the scene changes every time: front desk, patient visit, hands on a keyboard, a pet on an exam table.

athenahealth · Weave · SimplePractice · Deel · Hint

One treatment on every photo

A fixed graphic treatment (duotone, black and white, a brand shape) makes very different photos read as one series.

Loom · Rippling · NexHealth · Mercury

One real UI card

A single, legible interface card with specific content, never a cluster of placeholder bars.

Calendly · Loom · Jane · Hint

One big number

Data and list posts lead with the figure itself, set large.

Icons floating over a desk scene (the one cheap-looking card on Hint's and athenahealth's pages, and the genre of your current covers), and article titles baked into the image.

In your real listing card

Your card shows only the top 56% of each image (the title panel covers the rest), so every layout keeps faces, numbers and cards in that band. Same 12 articles, before and after, in a replica of the card.

Rules that keep it varied

  1. The category picks the layout; never choose by taste per post.
  2. Rotate the field colour (deep blue, primary blue, pale tint) so neighbouring cards differ.
  3. Never two headset portraits in a row. Every third cover is a Scene or a Split.
  4. One UI card at most, with specific, readable content. No placeholder bars.
  5. No corner checkmark, no glow streaks, no floating icon clusters, no title on the image.
  6. Faces, numbers and cards stay in the top 56% (x 115–1085, y 0–353 of a 1200×630 canvas).
  7. Numbers on covers are verified brand facts only (for example the $4–8 per hour range).

Photo brief for new images

Keep the people and the healthcare setting you like; change what the photos show and how they are shot.

  1. Subjects rotate: the VA at work (over the shoulder, hands on keyboard, on a call without smiling at the camera), the practice (front desk check-in, clinician with patient, empty operatory or exam room), and details (insurance card, appointment book, a pet on the exam table, a dental model).
  2. Style: documentary, natural window light, shallow depth of field, real-looking skin and clothes, a cool palette with blue present but not glowing.
  3. Never in the photo: floating UI, icons, checkmarks, light streaks, text, logos. The layout adds the brand.
  4. Composition: subject in the upper half, at 1200×630, with clean space on one side for the card or number.
