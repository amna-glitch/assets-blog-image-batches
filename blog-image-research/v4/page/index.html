Notes

"After" means the idea, not the artwork

Each style borrows a layout or technique from the named blog. None uses their artwork, logos or illustrations. Styles marked "Original" are mine, built for gaps in your categories.

Photos are stand-ins

Photo styles use crops of your current images, so old floating cards show at some edges. New photos without built-in UI will make them cleaner.

Needs a photographer or illustrator

Some of the best covers seen can't be templated: Squarespace and Zendesk editorial portraits, One Medical still-life objects, monday.com and Oscar character illustration. They're worth commissioning for flagship posts.

Seen, and avoided

Ahrefs-style YouTube thumbnails (shouting text over a face), Intercom-style full titles on the image, and icons floating over a desk scene. They look busy in a grid and repeat your current problem.

How to use 79 styles without chaos

  1. Pick 8 to 12 as your core set, at least one per family. Keep the rest for special posts.
  2. Map each article type to one or two styles, so the type decides the cover.
  3. No two neighbouring cards in the same family.
  4. Same palette, icon set and type on every cover. That's what keeps 79 styles looking like one brand.
  5. No prices or figures on covers until your team confirms them.
